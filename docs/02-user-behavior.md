# User Behavior

A safety engineer opens the app and picks the area they are
responsible for, either by choosing a city or county from a list or by
drawing an outline on the map. They see how crashes in that area split
across emphasis areas (the official TxDOT categories for crash types,
such as speeding or intersection crashes), and they compare that split
against the whole H-GAC region. They can also pick a similar county or
city to compare against, which shows them which crash types are worse
in their own area than elsewhere.

With that picture in hand, they build a list of locations worth
improving. They draw each one on the map: a line along a stretch of
road, or a point on an intersection. Locations can also come from an
uploaded file. Crash points and the reference overlay layers stay
visible while they draw, so they can see where crashes bunch up and
place their locations accordingly. Each location carries a buffer, a
distance outward from the drawn shape that decides which crashes count
as belonging to it, and the engineer can adjust that distance at any
time.

For each location on the list, they review its crash history and add
countermeasures. Each countermeasure is scored on its own, so they can
try several and compare. The expected crash reduction and the cost
score update as they type. They mark the option they prefer, and a bar
shows how much of the area's crash total the whole plan would address.

Finally they export a report covering the locations, their crash
history, the countermeasures considered, and the expected reductions,
in a form they can drop into a safety plan or a grant application.
